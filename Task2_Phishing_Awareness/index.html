CYBER SECURITY

Phishing Awareness Program

Learn to spot suspicious emails, fake login pages and fraudulent websites before they trick you.

1. Check the sender

Look beyond the display name. Verify the complete email address and watch for look-alike domains, unexpected replies and unusual urgency.

⚠ Suspicious: “Bank Support” <support@bank-secure-login.example>

2. Inspect links

Hover before clicking. A link that displays one destination but points to another is a major warning sign.

✓ Safer habit: Open the service using a bookmark or manually typed official address.

3. Fake login pages

Attackers copy familiar branding. Check the domain, HTTPS certificate details, spelling, unexpected prompts and whether the page asks for unusual information.

⚠ Red flag: A login page hosted on an unrelated domain.

4. Social engineering

  • Urgency and fear
  • Impersonation of trusted people
  • Fake prizes or refunds
  • Requests for passwords, OTPs or money

Case study

A finance employee receives an email claiming an invoice is overdue and asks them to open a document and sign in. The sender address is slightly misspelled and the link uses a different domain. The correct response is to avoid the link, verify the request independently and report the message.
